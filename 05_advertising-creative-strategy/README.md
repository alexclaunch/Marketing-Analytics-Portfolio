# Advertising Creative Strategy — Tootsie Campaign and Creative Work

**MKTG 3460: Advertising Creative Strategy** · Daniels College of Business · Spring 2026

The course covered how advertising gets made: writing a creative brief, building personas and a value proposition, choosing a media mix, and executing a consistent concept across channels. The term's main deliverable was an integrated campaign for a real brand, alongside individual assignments in ad critique, concept development, and content analysis.

## Tootsie Covers It All — integrated marketing campaign

A five-person team campaign for Tootsie built around one insight: consumers still think of Tootsie as the classic chocolate candy from their childhood and don't know it already makes the fruit and sour flavors they and their kids are chasing. The campaign's line, "Tootsie Covers It All," makes that point literally and through humor, with a running gag that "for the first time in 50 years, Mr. Owl tries something new."

The final paper works through a full creative brief, two research-backed personas (a Gen Z snack-seeker and a millennial mom who does the household shopping), a value proposition, design planning (tone, palette, typography, and how the brand system stays consistent across new and heritage products), and a five-channel media mix with executions for social, connected TV, out-of-home, in-store, and digital display. Every channel choice is tied to a cited reason the persona would be reached there.

My part of the team's work was designing and building the final presentation deck, which I made in Claude Design, and contributing to concept development during the brainstorming that produced the campaign idea.

## Individual work

**Blackstone ad critique and redesign.** Two real Blackstone Instagram ads taken apart and rebuilt. For each, the write-up explains what fails in the original (product renders with no human scenario, spec-sheet copy, generic urgency, a "Memorial Day" ad with nothing of Memorial Day in it) and then presents a redesigned version with a specific audience, a scene the viewer can picture themselves in, and a tagline that connects the product to a goal they already have.

**Photo ad concepts.** Five ads built from a set of assigned photographs, each pairing the image with a real brand and a deliberate tone: emotional for Match, energetic for Spotify, bold for Nike, minimalist for Patagonia, witty for Casper. Each mockup includes headline, brand mark, and tagline, followed by the concept and why it works for that brand's position in its category.

**Cup of Jo blog analysis.** A content and monetization analysis of the lifestyle blog Cup of Jo, examining how individual posts use tone, imagery, affiliate links, and community engagement, and what makes its sponsored and editorial content feel trustworthy to its audience.

**Ad diversity case write-up.** A case analysis of 1970s fast-food advertising aimed at Black consumers, on what those campaigns got right about representation, where their execution failed, and how advertisers can target specific communities authentically rather than superficially.

**Tools and methods:** creative briefs · persona development · value propositions · media planning · ad critique and redesign · concept and copy development · Claude Design
