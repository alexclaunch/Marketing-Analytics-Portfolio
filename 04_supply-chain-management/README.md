# Supply Chain Management — Global Food Waste, a DMAIC Project

**MKTG 3380 / MGMT 3700: Global Supply Chain Management** · Daniels College of Business · Spring 2026

A ten-week, data-driven project applying the DMAIC improvement cycle (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control) to global food waste, working from the raw 2019 FAO / Our World in Data dataset toward a supply chain strategy proposal. This was a team project; the data cleaning log and the analysis behind the presentations are the parts of the work most fully represented here.

## What the project involved

The dataset had to be cut down to a comparable set of countries before any analysis meant anything, and every removal was documented with its reasoning rather than simply dropped. The analysis then looked at distribution, variance, and coefficient of variation to separate real inefficiency from reporting artifact, and the findings were carried into a mid-point strategy and a final proposal.

The brief changed several times over the term, so a real part of the work was looping back through earlier DMAIC steps as the scope shifted: re-deciding what was in scope, what question was actually being answered, and whether the countries being compared belonged in the same set at all. The gap between the mid-point and final presentations is where the strategy matured.

**Tools and methods:** DMAIC · Excel (data cleaning, variance and CV analysis) · data validation · supply chain strategy · managerial presentation

## What I took away

Problems like this rarely have a closeable solution. Food waste across a global supply chain is moving and multi-causal, and the assignment itself kept evolving. DMAIC's value turned out to be the discipline of measuring, analyzing, and then continuing to keep tabs, and being willing to go back and rethink an earlier step when the data or the brief no longer supported where the work had landed.
